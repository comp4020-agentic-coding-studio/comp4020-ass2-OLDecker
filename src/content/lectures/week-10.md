The final project succeeds or struggles largely on the structure you pick in
this lecture's shadow. This lecture is about that choice: what makes a
structure rich enough to sustain a semester's-end analysis without being so
large or so poorly resolved that you spend the whole time fighting the data
instead of learning from it.

## Outline

- signs of a good project structure: a clear function, a resolvable region of
  interest, a method you can speak to
- signs of a bad one: too large to describe fully, too low-resolution to say
  anything precise, or already exhaustively studied with nothing left to ask
- how much scope actually fits in the time available
- pitching your choice in [this week's session](/sessions/10-project-pitch/)
