Most of a protein's surface is there to hold the rest of it together; usually
only a small region — an active site, a binding pocket, an interface — is
where the function actually happens. This lecture is about finding and
reading that region: what makes a pocket look like a pocket, how shape and
chemistry together determine what binds there, and a first look at docking as
a way of testing a hypothesis about a ligand computationally rather than only
experimentally.

## Outline

- recognising a pocket: shape, and the chemistry lining it
- catalytic residues vs structural ones — not every conserved residue is
  doing the same job
- protein–ligand interactions: hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contact, shape
  complementarity
- docking, in outline, as a hypothesis-testing tool rather than an oracle
