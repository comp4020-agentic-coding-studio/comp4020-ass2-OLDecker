Crystallography needs a crystal, which not every protein will form. Cryo-EM
and NMR don't. This lecture covers single-particle cryo-EM (freezing many
copies of a molecule and averaging over thousands of images) and solution NMR
(reading structure from how nuclei perturb each other's magnetic environment),
and what each is good and bad at compared to crystallography and to each
other.

## Outline

- cryo-EM: freezing, imaging, and averaging thousands of particles into one map
- why cryo-EM changed which proteins are solvable (large complexes, membrane
  proteins) — the "resolution revolution"
- NMR: structure from solution, as an ensemble rather than a single frozen
  pose
- a same-protein comparison across all three methods, ahead of this week's
  session
