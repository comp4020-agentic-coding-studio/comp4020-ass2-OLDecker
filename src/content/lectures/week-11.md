Every method this semester assumes a molecule holds still long enough to be
measured. Many of the most important proteins don't. This lecture looks at a
handful of cases where that assumption breaks — intrinsically disordered
regions that have no single fold, membrane proteins that resist
crystallisation, complexes that only exist transiently — as a check against
treating any one structure as the whole truth about a protein.

## Outline

- intrinsically disordered regions: proteins, or parts of proteins, with no
  single fold to solve
- membrane proteins: why they're historically under-represented in the PDB,
  and how cryo-EM changed that
- transient complexes and conformational change: a structure as one frame,
  not the whole film
- what this means for your final project's honesty about its own limits
