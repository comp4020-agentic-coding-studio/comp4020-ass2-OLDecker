Most of the structures in the PDB were solved by X-ray crystallography. This
lecture covers what that means well enough that a number like "1.8 Å
resolution" stops being a vague quality signal and starts being something you
can reason about: what has to happen physically for a crystal to give you a
structure, why the phase problem makes this hard, and what resolution and
B-factors do and don't tell you about how much to trust a given atom's
position.

## Outline

- growing and diffracting a crystal, in outline
- the phase problem, without the maths
- reading resolution: what changes between 1.0 Å and 3.0 Å
- B-factors as a per-atom confidence signal, and where they're highest
- what a crystal structure cannot show you (crystal contacts, missing loops,
  a single frozen conformation)
